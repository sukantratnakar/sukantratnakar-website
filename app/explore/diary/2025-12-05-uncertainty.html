Uncertainty Creates Its Own Story

change-management uncertainty communication

I was buying something on Facebook Marketplace this week. Everything looked normal until the seller said he preferred $20 cash "due to privacy concerns."

That line made me pause. In Canada, Interac is one of the safest payment systems we have. It doesn't expose your banking details. It simply shows your name and the email or phone number you choose. There's no real privacy risk.

But this moment reminded me of something deeper that I often see in change management:
People don't resist change. They resist uncertainty.

When someone is unsure, they fill the gap with assumptions. And assumptions create hesitation, even when the new option is clearly safer or more efficient.

In organizations, we see the same pattern. A new process, a new tool, or a new way of working can trigger discomfort—not because it is risky, but because it is unfamiliar. The story we tell ourselves becomes the barrier.

The real work of change is not pushing a new system. It is reducing uncertainty through clarity, trust, and simple communication. When people understand the "why," they stop resisting the "how."

A small Marketplace chat became a reminder:
If we don't explain the change, people will explain it to themselves—and that story is rarely accurate.
