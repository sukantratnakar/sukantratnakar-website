The Sunfish Paradox

Strategy Leadership

What This "Stupid" Fish Teaches Us About Thriving in Business

I came across a YouTube reel about the ocean sunfish the other day, and I haven't been able to stop thinking about it.

By every conventional measure, this creature shouldn't exist. It's slow. It looks awkward. It swims without grace. And here's the kicker: it has one of the smallest brain-to-body ratios of any vertebrate. For a fish that can weigh over 2,000 kg, its brain is roughly the size of a walnut.

Yet it's survived for 50 million years.

What the sunfish teaches us:

  • We don't need perfection to succeed. The sunfish isn't trying to be a shark. It's not competing on speed or intelligence. It found its niche and mastered it.
  • Overthinking kills momentum. With that tiny brain, the sunfish can't overthink. It acts on instinct, follows simple patterns, and executes consistently.
  • Scale and presence matter. The sunfish isn't fast, but its sheer size protects it. In business, this is your brand trust, your network, your market presence.
  • Build partnerships, not solo empires. Sunfish survive by letting smaller fish clean them. They know their limits.
  • Bet on volume, not perfection. Sunfish produce up to 300 million eggs at once. Most won't make it, but that's the strategy.
  • Conserve energy for what matters. Sunfish float and conserve energy. They understand something we forget: constant motion isn't the same as progress.

You don't need to be the smartest in the room. You just need to focus on what actually sustains you, execute relentlessly on the basics, and stop measuring yourself against someone else's definition of excellence.
