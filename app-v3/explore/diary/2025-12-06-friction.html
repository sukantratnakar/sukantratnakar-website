Friction Doesn't Hurt the Company. It Hurts the Public.

systems friction trust

Yesterday I ran into the same problem I faced two months ago. The QR code on this parking stand still hasn't been fixed, and even the app refuses to load the correct location. Nothing has changed.

I was visiting Ashleigh Mattern for a meeting, and before walking in, I spent almost ten minutes in minus 21 trying different ways to pay. Not because the system works well, but because not paying would cost me more in penalties.

And that's the real point here. A broken QR code doesn't reduce the company's revenue. If anything, the friction can lead to more tickets, and a ticket is worth more than a parking fee. So the company doesn't feel the loss. The public does.

The management lesson is simple: when you make things easy, people do the right thing without stress. When you build friction into a system, you might still make money, but you lose trust along the way.
